How the program works

Terms. Each term has 12 teaching weeks and 1 break week. Week 6 is the midterm, week 12 is finals and Demo Day. If you can only study 4 hours a day, make every term 6 months. A plan you can sustain beats one that looks fast.

Grading. Finals and problem sets 40%, midterm 20%, project 40%. Pass mark is 70%. Below 70%, repeat the weak chapters before moving on. Ask a peer or mentor to check at least the project grade, because self-grading drifts upward.

The 30-minute rule. Free AI chat tools are fine for learning, but sit with a problem for 30 minutes first, and ask for explanations, not finished code.

Hardware. Train models on free Google Colab or Kaggle notebooks, not on an old PC. Everything else here runs on a modest laptop and a browser. An SSD is the best upgrade you can make.

Honest limit. This gives you skills and a public portfolio, not an accredited degree. For software and AI work, the portfolio is what people check. Verify each link is still live before the term starts.

Working method, from day one. Keep notes in markdown in a Git repo, in your own words. Keep a decision log: what you chose, what you rejected, why. Review with Anki. State a hypothesis before debugging, change one thing at a time, and record what did not work. Talk through your work out loud in English, and record a 5-minute technical explanation every week.
